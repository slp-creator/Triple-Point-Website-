What is speech therapy?

Therapy built around your specific goals.

Speech therapy addresses the way sounds, words, and language are produced and understood. Lauren provides individualized treatment for articulation errors, fluency, and overall communication clarity — whether the goal is correcting a specific sound error, improving intelligibility, or building confidence speaking in everyday situations.

Every plan starts with a thorough evaluation and is built around the client's own goals — there is no one-size-fits-all program. Sessions are collaborative, practical, and designed to translate into real, everyday communication.

Speech and myofunctional patterns are often connected. When appropriate, Lauren integrates orofacial myofunctional therapy techniques to address the underlying muscle function driving certain speech sound errors.

Who is a good candidate?

Children and adults with articulation errors, speech sound disorders, stuttering, or a general desire to improve communication clarity and confidence.

How long does treatment take?

Timelines vary by goal and age. Many clients begin with weekly sessions, reducing in frequency as progress is made.

Is there a referral required?

No referral is required. Lauren is happy to coordinate with pediatricians, teachers, or other providers as needed.

What we treat

Conditions addressed through speech therapy.

The following concerns are commonly addressed through individualized speech therapy sessions.

Speech Sound Disorders
  • Articulation and phonological disorders in children
  • Mumbled or unclear speech
  • Lisps and other individual sound distortions
  • Difficulty being understood by unfamiliar listeners
Fluency

Treatment of fluency is based on a possible underlying myofunctional disorder. If an underlying myofunctional disorder is found, Lauren will help reestablish functional movement of the tongue and lips, along with breath support.

Communication clarity
  • Overall speech intelligibility
  • Communication confidence in social and professional settings
  • Carryover of clear speech into everyday conversation
Related myofunctional factors
  • Speech errors linked to tongue tie or restricted oral movement
  • Tongue thrust patterns affecting certain sounds
  • Oral habits contributing to unclear speech

The bigger picture

When speech and myofunctional patterns intersect.

Speech development is a very complex process, and assessing the underlying muscle function of the tongue, lips, and jaw is essential. Orofacial myofunctional therapy can help address the root cause alongside traditional speech techniques.

Lauren's dual background in speech therapy and myofunctional therapy means these connections are identified early, so treatment addresses the full picture rather than just the symptom.
