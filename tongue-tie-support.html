What is tongue tie support?

Tongue tie affects far more than just feeding.

Tongue tie (ankyloglossia) and lip tie occur when the band of tissue connecting the tongue or lip is unusually short, tight, or thick — restricting normal range of motion. Left unaddressed, this can affect feeding, speech, breathing, and sleep at every age, from newborns struggling to latch to adults with unexplained jaw tension.

Lauren provides thorough evaluation to determine whether a tongue or lip tie is contributing to your concerns, and — when a release procedure is recommended — works alongside your provider to prepare the oral muscles beforehand and guide recovery afterward.

A release alone does not retrain the muscles. Myofunctional therapy before and after release helps ensure the tissue heals correctly and that new, functional movement patterns actually take hold.

Who is a good candidate?

Infants, children, and adults with a diagnosed or suspected tongue or lip tie — whether you're considering a release, have one scheduled, or have already had the procedure.

Is a release always necessary?

No. Not every tie requires release. Lauren's evaluation helps determine whether therapy alone is sufficient or whether a referral for release is appropriate.

Is there a referral required?

No referral is required. Lauren coordinates directly with tongue tie–informed dentists, ENTs, and lactation consultants as part of your care team.

Signs & symptoms

How tongue and lip tie can show up.

Tongue and lip tie can present differently depending on age. The following are common signs that a tie may be contributing to feeding, speech, or breathing difficulties.

Infants
  • Difficulty latching or staying latched while breast or bottle feeding
  • Slow weight gain
  • Clicking sounds or milk leakage while feeding
  • Reflux-like symptoms and excessive gassiness
  • Maternal nipple pain or mastitis
Children
  • Picky eating or difficulty with certain food textures
  • Speech sound errors
  • Mouth breathing and open-mouth resting posture
  • Snoring or restless sleep
  • Dental crowding or orthodontic concerns
Adults
  • Jaw tension, TMJ pain, or chronic neck/shoulder tightness
  • Difficulty with certain speech sounds
  • Difficulty clearing food from teeth
  • Chronic mouth breathing or sleep-disordered breathing
Pre & post-release
  • Preparation to maximize range of motion before a release procedure
  • Guided stretches to prevent tissue reattachment after release
  • Retraining the tongue and lips to use their new functional range of motion and strength
  • Scar management and desensitization exercises
